Phase A · design direction

A miniature that sleeps, wakes and keeps the books

Reference research, three directions and a recommendation for the dashboard described in the revised brief. No code yet. This page is for choosing a direction.

Revised 2026-11-1713 references, each opened and checkedAwaiting owner approval

01Creative vision

Most infrastructure visuals show a system that is always on. This platform's point is the opposite: it spends most of its life switched off, and every minute of GPU is a deliberate, paid decision. The dashboard should make that feel physical. At rest it is a dark, quiet model on a desk. When a job arrives, light travels through it, machines unfold one after another, and when the work is done they fold away again while a small ledger shows what the visit cost.

That gives a recruiter a story they can follow in a minute (it sleeps to save money, it wakes itself, it checks its own work) and gives an engineer a precise instrument (every light is a real event with a timestamp). It reads as product craft rather than a sci-fi poster, which is also what the owner's existing renders already look like.

03Three directions

Direction A · recommended

Night Shift Workshop

Identity
A single isometric diorama on a navy desk, built in the language of the owner's renders: flat-shaded lavender machines, thin bright edges, aqua and violet indicator lights. Two raised platforms stand for the two Availability Zones, joined by a short bridge. Tilt-shift blur makes it read as a physical miniature.
Components
Machines on a workbench: arch (API Gateway), relay switch (Lambda), sorter trays (queues), bins (DLQs), open crate (S3), tower with roof modules (control plane and pods), empty cradle that receives a cassette (GPU node), drive that docks to it (model EBS). Full map in section 05.
Data
Each job is a lavender ticket that slides along engraved rails. It gets stamped by LAYA, waits in trays, crosses the bridge to the GPU platform, and comes back as a document dropped into the crate.
Activation
Mechanical and sequential: lights climb a tower, a cassette slides into a cradle, a drive docks with a click of light. A global "awake" blend raises the ambient light when the control plane runs.
Interaction
Orthographic camera in fixed iso angles with 90° turns, three zoom stops, click-to-inspect.
Strengths
Reuses the existing art direction; built from boxes, so cheap to make procedurally; the clearest mapping of one object per component; very readable on phones.
Weaknesses
Risk of looking like a toy if the motion is cute rather than precise. Mitigation: mechanical easing, no bouncing, honest timing.
Build cost
Low to medium. Day one is realistic.
Direction B

Tide Harbour

Identity
A small night harbour seen from above. Water level and light carry the mood: dark and still when idle, lit quays when working.
Components
Harbour gate (API Gateway), pilot boat (Lambda), anchorage (workflow queue), customs house (LAYA), dry dock with crane (GPU ASG and node), lighthouse (EventBridge pre-warm), warehouse (S3), wreck buoy (DLQ).
Data
Jobs are boats that queue, get inspected, and either turn back (rejected) or enter the dry dock for work.
Activation
Lights come on along the quay, the crane lifts a hull into the dock, the lighthouse sweeps at 06:55.
Interaction
Free orbit with a low horizon, guided camera along the waterline.
Strengths
Very approachable for non-engineers; queues and waiting feel natural on water.
Weaknesses
The metaphor strains for KEDA, the Cluster Autoscaler and pods; engineers must translate everything. Water and boats need modelling and shaders, and it discards the existing renders.
Build cost
High. Not a one-day prototype.
Direction C

Living Schematic

Identity
A 2.5D circuit board seen almost from above. Components are chips and modules on a dark board; copper traces carry signals; power rails visibly charge and drain.
Components
Each AWS resource is a labelled chip; pods are small chips in a socket on the control-plane module; the GPU is a large chip in an empty socket that is filled when the node joins.
Data
Pulses run along traces; queue depth is a stack of LEDs; failures short to an amber fuse (DLQ).
Activation
Power flows from the always-on rail into each zone; unpowered regions fade to the board's base colour.
Interaction
Pan and zoom like a map, hover to trace a signal path end to end.
Strengths
Most precise for engineers; trivial on phones; could run as SVG or canvas with no WebGL at all.
Weaknesses
Close to a diagram with animation, which the brief asks to avoid. Less memorable.
Build cost
Low.

04Recommendation: Night Shift Workshop

It is the only direction that is memorable, accurate and buildable in a day, and it continues work the owner has already done. Two ideas are borrowed from the others: the power-rail idea from C becomes thin glowing lines engraved in the desk that show which zones are powered, and the 06:55 pre-warm from B keeps its own small ritual (the clock's hand sweeps and taps the relay).

Night desk Asleep machine Lavender face Aqua · active Violet · GPU Amber · retry
  • Layout: left to right in the order work flows. Front edge: Ingress (arch, relay, clock). Middle: Buffer (two sorter trays, two bins, crate). Back-left platform: zone 1a with the control-plane tower. Back-right platform: zone 1c with the GPU cradle and model drive. The bridge between the platforms is the cross-AZ link.
  • Rules of light: aqua means a CPU-side component is working, violet means GPU work, amber means a retry or warning, a dashed or breathing outline means the state is inferred rather than observed. Nothing glows without an event behind it.
  • Motion rules: mechanical ease-in-out, no overshoot; long real waits (boot, model load, cooldowns) are shown as rings that fill, and are compressed in replay with a visible marker.
  • The ledger: a small panel on the desk corner showing "compute running now" and "this month: billed vs if always on". It expands into the cost drawer.

06Storyboard: a cold job that needs the GPU

Durations come from the platform's configuration. Boot and model-load times are placeholders until real traces are recorded.

  1. BeforeAsleep. Desk dark. Only the always-on pieces show a faint edge: arch, relay, trays, crate, clock. Ledger reads "compute running now: $0.00/h".
  2. 0 sA request arrives. A ticket appears at the arch and the /jobs route light flashes.
  3. +0.1 sRelay closes. The ticket drops into the workflow tray. A pulse runs along the desk rail to the tower: start the control plane.
  4. +~1 minTower wakes. The scene's light rises. Panels climb; the status window turns aqua. Roof modules rise one by one as pods become ready (drawn as inferred until the exporter exists).
  5. nextWorker takes the ticket; LAYA scans it. The scanner arm stamps it. Here the story can branch: decision-only jobs finish on CPU, rejected ones go straight to the crate with a grey stamp, and the GPU platform never lights.
  6. nextHand-off to the GPU tray. The ticket crosses to the violet tray at the bridge. Within 15 s the KEDA lever flips and a dashed vLLM outline appears in the empty cradle (pod pending).
  7. +~10 sCapacity requested. The autoscaler arm extends from the tower to the cradle. ASG desired 0 → 1.
  8. 90–150 s GPU start (not counting the tower's wake-up above)The cassette arrives. It slides into the cradle, the model drive docks with a line of light, a cable links back across the bridge (node joined), and the core fills as qwen-general loads.
  9. secondsGeneration. The ticket travels worker → LiteLLM → bridge → core. The violet stripe runs while tokens stream back as small dots.
  10. nextVerification. Back through the LAYA scanner. A failed check sends it round again with a small regeneration pip; a pass earns an aqua check.
  11. nextResult stored. A document drops into the crate. The ledger adds this job's estimated cost.
  12. +120 sGPU winds down. A cooldown ring closes around the core; the core empties; after 2 more minutes the autoscaler arm pulls the cassette out. GPU meter stops.
  13. +30 minBack to sleep. The watchdog dial fills, the tower's panels fall top-down, the room dims, and the guard latch on the cradle snaps shut. Caption: "GPU paid for 6 min today. Always-on would have cost …"

Other endings

  • Error and retry: the ticket turns amber, returns to its tray and gains a receive-count pip; on the third failure it drops into the bin below.
  • Needs review: after the last failed verification the document lands in the crate with a hollow amber mark.
  • Warm path: a second job inside the cooldown goes straight to the core; the ring resets.
  • Spot interruption (once Spot is on): the cassette's edge flashes amber with a 2-minute ring; the ticket goes back to the tray.
  • Pre-warm with no work: the clock taps the relay, the tower wakes, nothing arrives, and the watchdog walks it back to sleep.
  • Launch failure: the arm extends but no cassette comes; the cradle shows an amber "no capacity" tag and the ticket waits.

07Interaction design

Camera

Orthographic iso view. Drag rotates within ±25°; buttons turn in 90° steps. Three zoom stops: whole desk, one platform, one object. Selecting an object eases the camera to frame it; Esc returns.

Inspector

Opens from the side on desktop, as a bottom sheet on phones. Two tabs: "What it does" in plain words, "How it works" with real config values, recent events and metrics. Never shows IDs or payloads.

Live

A status line at the top: "Asleep since 03:12 UTC" or "Working · 2 jobs · GPU ready". When asleep it offers "Watch the last run". Stale telemetry is shown as stale, not as asleep.

Replay

Run picker by date and outcome. Bottom timeline with event markers in the light colours; compressed gaps drawn as a break. Speed 1×, 4×, 15×, 60×. Captions above the timeline; the event log mirrors them as text.

Guided tour

Plays the best recent run with a scripted camera, 60–90 s, written for non-engineers. Skippable at any time and ends in Explore.

History

The ledger drawer: monthly billed cost, GPU active vs idle hours, jobs by outcome, cost per job. Clicking a day opens its runs in Replay.

08What this means for the build

  • Rendering in 3D (R3F) with an orthographic camera, rather than 2D sprites: the renders are references, objects are procedural boxes, so camera turns and zoom stay crisp at any size.
  • Global awake value drives ambient light and palette blend; each object reads its own state from the shared reducer and eases toward it.
  • Tilt-shift and outline effects are optional and disabled on phones and in reduced-motion mode.
  • The 12 new objects in section 05 are part of the day-one kit. Each is a few boxes, a light and one moving part.
  • The SVG first frame (GitHub globe pattern) doubles as the no-WebGL fallback later.
